# 10. PRECEDENTS

### What has already been tried — and what exactly broke

---

## 0. Why this file

The project is not the first. **Everything we propose, someone has already done — in pieces.** A permanent figure in a house. A village of houses. Collective upbringing. A company town as a family. Dense transmission of a norm. Old people raising grandchildren.

**The project's duty is not to reinvent but to look at what broke.** Especially among those who came closest.

---

## 1. SOS Children's Villages (1949 – …): the nearest relative, and it must be read slowly

### What it is

A permanent "mother" lives with the children in a **house**; the houses form a **village.** Not a barracks, not an institution — a family and a community. **This is literally a description of our node**, running for seventy-five years: some 137 countries, around 550 facilities, tens of thousands of staff, over a million children across its history.

### What worked

- **A permanent figure living with the children** — that is M1, and the model **scaled worldwide.** So the form is viable: the node is not a utopia.
- **A house, not a ward. A village, not a facility.** The direction is right.

### What broke

This must be listed in full, because each item is a safeguard we are obliged to have.

- **The founder, Hermann Gmeiner, was himself an abuser.** Disclosed in October 2025: eight documented internal cases, compensation paid between 2013 and 2023. The Austrian association was suspended from membership of the federation.
- **Systematic abuse in roughly twenty facilities** across Africa and Asia from the 1990s onward: physical, sexual and emotional abuse, sexual exploitation, grooming, neglect.
- **"Functional impunity"** — the phrase used by the independent commission that investigated the organization.
- **Whistleblowers were dismissed. Children's word was doubted.**
- **Children who reported abuse were "exited" from the villages — as retribution.**
- **Austria, Moosburg, 2008–2020:** children were beaten, locked up, photographed naked. A detailed internal report from 2020 **was never made public.**
- **Suriname:** abuse from the village's founding (1972) to its closure (2006) — **more than thirty years.**
- **Syria:** children were separated from their families under pressure from the regime, transferred to the organization's centres, and some were later returned to the authorities **with no follow-up on their fate.**
- **Corruption:** contracts awarded to relatives of officials.

### The diagnosis — and it is not ours but their own analysts'

> **"A culture of silence." When an institution is regarded as sacred, criticism becomes almost impossible, creating a fertile environment in which abuse persists. The halo of gratitude around the institution makes it easy for children to disappear.**

And the conclusion the independent analyses reached: **what is needed are models that empower families and communities as sites of protection, rather than replacing them with institutional care.**

### What follows for Foundation — line by line

| SOS failure | What answers it in the protocol |
|---|---|
| **A sacred founder who turned out to be an abuser** | **M7: no center, no founder, no sacred figure.** The Second Foundation is rejected explicitly (`README`). There is nothing to sanctify. |
| **A culture of silence in a closed world** | **The annual exchange** (`09_VIRTUES` §3): every year the child lives in **another node**, among other people, in another faith. **He has adults outside the institution who know him personally.** The closed world does not exist, by construction. |
| **Whistleblowers dismissed** | **M8: exit is free and unpunished.** A node that punishes leaving falls out of the network. |
| **Children who reported abuse were expelled** | **M5 + M12: another node is obliged to take them.** To expel a child is **to hand him to people who will ask why he left.** Expulsion stops being a punishment and becomes evidence. |
| **Internal report suppressed** | **M10: the method is open. M3: ruptures are public and stated.** Concealment shows up as the absence of what ought to be there. |
| **Capture by a state (Syria)** | **Honestly: not fully solved.** M6 (no dominant funder) does not save you from a state. The graph sees, breaks communion, and the children are caught (M5, M12) — **if the state releases them.** The limit is acknowledged and carried into `17_RISKS`. |
| **Corruption** | Not addressed by a separate rule (that would be content). A corrupt node **loses on the long trace** and is washed out by selection. A weak answer — named as weak. |
| **Seventy-five years — and no data on what became of the children** | **M9: the long trace.** ← **The entire case for M9 in one line.** |

### And the essential difference SOS could not admit

> **SOS replaced the family. Foundation restores the village around the family.**

The apex at SOS was a **hired "mother"**, an employee of the organization. The apex in Foundation is **the child's grandmother** (`07_ECONOMY` §2.4-bis), and she leaves only together with him (M1). **The institution does not raise the child. The institution keeps the village in which the grandmother raises her grandchild.**

### The requirement that follows from SOS directly

**A child's cry must travel outward — along the communion graph.**

The child's AI belongs to **the child**, not the node (`08_DATA` §3) — so the node cannot silence it. And the child has **living people in other nodes who know him personally** (the exchange, `09_VIRTUES` §3).

**This is not a hotline. It is relationships.** Exactly what SOS children did not have in any of seventy-five years.

## 2. The kibbutzim (1910 – …)

**What they did:** a daytime collective, professional caregivers, shared domestic life — and **communal night sleep, apart from the parents.**

**What worked:** distributed daytime care, early socialization, multiple caregivers. Along these axes children developed normally or better than peers. **Direct evidence in favour of allo-parenting.**

**What broke:** **the night.** Attachment research showed an elevated share of insecure attachment among communal-sleeping children. **The kibbutzim themselves** abandoned it and returned children to the family for the night — a movement led largely by **the mothers themselves** (`05_ATTACHMENT` §3).

**What this proves, and it matters more than the fact itself:** **the system measured itself and corrected.** It did not defend the uniform — **it changed.** That is evolution working as intended, and it is what SOS did not do in seventy-five years.

---

## 3. Makarenko (1920–1935)

**What he did:** the collective, real productive labour, adolescent self-government, "as much respect for a person as possible and as much demand upon him as possible."

**Result:** by testimony and later estimate — around 3,000 pupils returned to full lives. **[estimate, not measurement]** — the figure is disputed; there is no long-trace data.

**What we take:** the collective as educator, real work, self-government.

**What does not transfer:** the emergency context (war, famine, mass homelessness), the totalitarian frame, and the absence of a permanent apex for most of the children. This was **emergency medicine, not a way of life.**

---

## 4. Orphanages and deprivation: what happens without an apex

Spitz; the Romanian orphans of the Ceaușescu regime (the ERA study). **There was care, food, a roof — and no permanent figure.**

The result: developmental delay, persistent damage to the capacity to form attachment, some of which **is not compensated even by subsequent adoption into good families.**

> **This is what M1 forbids. Not "undesirable" — a measured irreversibility.**

---

## 5. British boarding schools

Early severance from the family in a closed, single-sex environment. A persistent pattern of damage to the adult capacity for intimacy is described (the term is contested; the phenomenon is documented).

**What it confirms:** what harms is **not the building and not the collective, but the severance.** Another independent line of evidence for M1 — and a direct reason why a node cannot be a closed single-sex environment (`03_SOVEREIGNTY`).

---

## 6. Haredim, Amish, Hutterites

**What works:** high fertility, dense transmission of a norm, persistence across generations. **The mechanism is real.**

**The price:** the narrowing of alternatives, above all women's. **It is precisely this that makes the mechanism work — and precisely this that makes it a dead end** (`07_ECONOMY` §1).

**The project's position:** take the mechanism (the network, distributed care, the status of motherhood), **reject the shell** (the narrowing of alternatives). Whether the mechanism works without the shell is **the project's central untested bet.**

---

## 7. Japanese company towns

The firm as an extended family: company housing, company sports, lifetime employment, a doctor and a school on site. **It worked for decades.** It died with the bubble.

**What we take:** the form is **already proven** — a "village with a payroll" is viable, and it is culturally native to the target market (`07_ECONOMY` §2-bis).

**What we fix:** **illiquidity.** You could not leave the company — so the company did not have to be good. Free graduates (M11 + the second virtue) thaw it.

---

## 8. Grandparents: the species norm, not an experiment

**Millions of children around the world are right now being raised by their grandparents** — with living parents who left, who work far away, who could not or would not. Eastern Europe, China, Africa, Latin America, the United States.

**There are no demonstrations against this.** It is normal, and it always was.

Humans are the only species with a long post-reproductive life, and the best explanation (the grandmother hypothesis) is that **the old raised the grandchildren.** The isolated nuclear family killed that allo-parent.

> **In this respect Foundation is not a radical experiment but a restoration.**

| **The kibbutzim** | The daytime collective **works**; nighttime severance **breaks**; a system **can correct itself** |

| **Boarding schools** | It is the severance that harms, not the building |

| **Company towns** | A "village with a payroll" is a workable form; illiquidity killed it |

## 10. The honest status

**Not one precedent provides what is needed:** a documented method + machine data + a traced outcome. All of them are **testimony, not measurement.** SOS cannot say what became of its children, after seventy-five years. The kibbutzim measured one thing (attachment) — and corrected. Makarenko left a book, not a dataset.

**That hole is exactly what the project aims at** (`08_DATA` §0). Not "we thought of something better." But **"no one has ever looked at what came out."**

---

**The formula of this file.** It has all been tried, and almost all of it broke in the same place: **in closedness.** A closed institution with a sanctified founder produces a culture of silence, and a culture of silence produces vanished children — for seventy-five years, across a hundred and thirty-seven countries, with the very best of intentions. **The one thing no one has tried is openness: the method in plain view, the child with a voice of his own, and living people in five other nodes who know him by name.**
