# 09. THE VIRTUES

### Proposed, not prescribed · The two axes of the boundary

---

## 0. Status: proposed, not imposed

The network **does not dictate content** (`03_SOVEREIGNTY`). It has no canon of upbringing and cannot have one — otherwise a center would appear to interpret it.

Therefore the virtues are **proposed.** A node is free to adopt, alter or reject them; no one breaks communion over their absence. They will spread by **ordinary selection**: nodes that adopt them and thrive on the long trace (M9) will be copied. Nodes that reject them and thrive will prove us wrong.

**These are not commandments. They are hypotheses cast as rituals.**

---

## 1. Why exactly these: the two axes of the boundary

`06_BOUNDARY` established: **the living holds at the boundary** between two deaths.

- **Collapse into order** (I → everything, J → 0): obsession, addiction, dogma, the cage. One mode crowds out the rest; the world stops entering.
- **Collapse into chaos** (J → everything, I → 0): dissolution; nothing is retained; no form lasts.

So a child needs **two capacities, and they are opposites**: to hold himself **and** to let the world in. Either without the other is insufficient — each alone kills.

**The two virtues are those two axes, operationalized as children's rituals:**

| Virtue | Rhythm | Axis | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The erasure game** | daily / weekly | **I** | the child disposes of what remains of him. **Holds form.** |
| **Entering another world** | annually | **Δτ·J** | the child enters a life arranged otherwise. **Does not freeze.** |

Not a list of good qualities. **A complete set of two, covering both deaths.**

---

## 2. The first virtue: the erasure game (the I axis)

In full: `08_DATA` §2. Briefly:

**The child regularly plays a game whose currency is the erasure of the record about himself.** Time spent is time erased. Bonuses, prizes. An ordinary children's game, played because it is fun.

The child **does not study** privacy as a separate skill. He **plays** — and therefore knows, without memorizing:

1. a record of him **exists** (it cannot be hidden from him — the game is built on showing it);
2. the record **belongs to him**;
3. erasing is **normal, easy and pleasant.**

**What this actually is:** the daily management of his own Markov blanket. The child **does not learn a right — he grows an organ.**

**Honesty is mandatory:** **everything is erased, including the derived numbers.** If the raw is erased while the numbers have already left and are irrevocable, the child has learned that his sovereignty is **decorative** — and a **false boundary is worse than none.** The cost is real: the node loses data. That is exactly why **what is left is valid.**

---

## 3. The second virtue: the annual entry into another world (the J axis)

> **Every year the child enters a world genuinely arranged otherwise. And lives in it — does not look at it.**

Like the first, it is **proposed, not prescribed.** The network has no right to require it. A node that does not practise it violates nothing; it is simply placing a different bet, and in twenty years the long trace (M9) will show whose was right.

### 3.1. Not "another country" but another way of life

The canonical form is another country. But the essence is not distance.

A child at a resort **brings his own world with him**: the same hotel, the same people, the same food, a screen in his hand. He has let nothing in. **Tourism is not J but a stage set of J.**

**What is needed is another way of life.** Another faith, another rhythm, other rules, another poverty or another wealth, another language, another God or none. **Otherness matters, not kilometres.** For a city child, a village can be farther away than Tokyo.

### 3.2. The mechanism: exchange between nodes

The network **already is** a set of nodes with incompatible faiths (`03_SOVEREIGNTY`). That is ready-made infrastructure, and it gives the exchange three properties no ordinary trip has: **reciprocity, cheapness and trust.**

**A child from a Japanese techno-node spends a month at a religious node in Poland. A child from a secular rationalist node — at a national-cultural one. A city child — in a village.**

You **live in a family, not in a hotel.** You eat their food, rise by their clock, attend their feasts, do their work, sleep under their rules. This is **not a trip. It is an entry.**

**The exchange is reciprocal** — you host, you are hosted. Nobody pays for lodging; the cost is nearly zero.

### 3.3. The age ladder — derived from M1, not invented

**The attachment axiom outranks the virtue.** A month away from the apex, for a small child, is **a violation of M1**, not an education. Hence the ladder, and it follows strictly from the axiom:

| Age | Form of exchange | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **0 – ~6** | **The world comes to the child.** He goes nowhere; children and families from other nodes live in his home. | The apex is non-detachable (M1). But the exchange is **reciprocal** — so someone is always hosting. The small child gets J **without leaving the apex.** Simple and elegant: the other world enters the house. |
| **~7 – 12** | **Short trips to those who have already stayed with you.** Weeks, not months. The host is a family the child already knows. | The relationship is built **before** departure. The child goes not to strangers but to people who slept in his house. |
| **13+** | **Long and far.** A month, a summer, a year. Maximal otherness. | The form is assembled, the apex is interiorized, one can go far. |

**J is never bought at the price of I.** First there must be someone to let the world in; then the world is let in.

### 3.4. The exchange runs along the communion graph — and this changes what communion means

**A child is sent only to a node with which you are in full communion** (M3). Not to an "approved list" — **you keep the list yourself**, and you have already vouched for that node: you take its data, you are obliged to catch its children if it falls (M5), you are named in its will (M12).

And then this happens.

Today "being in communion" means: we exchange data, we insure one another, we recognize each other's methods. **That is bookkeeping.**

**The exchange of children restores communion's original meaning.** Eucharistic communion meant: **I will receive the sacrament in your church.** Here:

> **I will let my child sleep in your house.**

**The strongest form of recognition that exists among human beings.** And it raises the bar at a stroke: **communion is no longer entered lightly** — entering means you would trust them with your child. Communion ceases to be a tick on a list and becomes **what you risk your dearest on.**

And so with rupture: to break communion is to say "I will no longer let my child come to you." That is a **costly signal**, and everyone hears it (`02_COMMUNION` §5).

### 3.5. Exit is free — otherwise it is not an exchange but a cage

**The child may come home at any moment, at once, without explanation and without shame.** This is M8 at a child's scale, and it must be **physically real, not declarative:**

- the ticket home **in the child's own hands** from day one;
- contact with the apex — always, without the hosts' permission;
- **"I want to go home" is honoured immediately and without persuasion.** To talk him into staying is to turn an exchange into detention.

**If leaving is hard, it is not an exchange but a cage.** And then the host node has violated the very essence of the virtue, and the sending node is obliged to see it and break communion.

### 3.6. Four functions of one ritual

**First — J for the child.** He receives, annually, proof that **his world is not the only one.** Not an idea he was told — a **fact he lived.**

**Second — anti-capture.** M11 (no one owns a human being) becomes **a physical fact of biography rather than a clause in a charter.**

> **You cannot cage a person who has seen, every year, that it can be otherwise.**

He knows the cage is not the only world — **not because he was told, but because he has been outside.** A node, a corporation, a sect or a family wishing to raise a person "unable to want anything else" **will not be able to**, so long as the virtue is kept. This is exactly what makes the corporate market (`07_ECONOMY` §2-bis) safe: **Sony cannot raise a captive, because its child lives with others every year.**

**Third — the bridges of the network.** `02_COMMUNION` §4: **bridges are the most valuable thing in the graph**; they carry practice between "faiths" and keep the network connected **without a center.** And so:

> **The children who have lived in other nodes *are* the bridges.**

The exchange is **not a side effect but the mechanism of connectivity itself.** The child returns with their practices, their language, their people. Twenty years on this is **a person with his own people in five nodes** — and such a person will not let the network fall apart into warring churches.

**Fourth — liquidity.** A graduate who knows five nodes knows five worlds: markets, industries, corporations. **He is mobile because he can see** — and the mobility of graduates is exactly what turns the corporate market from capture into competition (`07_ECONOMY` §2-bis).

### 3.7. The network's pulse: exchange as measurable health

**The volume and diversity of exchanges is a direct indicator of the graph's health**, and it must be counted:

- **how many** children travel;
- **how far across the graph** (an exchange with a neighbouring, near-identical node is not J but self-deception);
- **how different** the faiths of sender and host are.

**A fall in exchanges = the network is calcifying into separate churches.** This is an early death signal, visible long before a schism. A node whose children visit only nodes exactly like itself **is deceiving itself — and it is visible from outside.**

> **The exchange is a pulse. Count it the way one counts a pulse.**

### 3.8. What can go wrong

- **[risk] Exchange as elite tourism.** Rich nodes exchange with rich nodes and it becomes a finishing school. **This kills the whole point.** Cured by the measurement in §3.7: graph distance and difference of faith are public.
- **[risk] Exchange as recruitment.** The host node poaches children and families. Cured by the fact that **the child returns and the apex does not move**; moving to another node is a separate decision, taken with the apex and not under the impression of a month.
- **[risk] Trauma.** See §3.3 (the age ladder) and §3.5 (free exit). Violate either and the virtue becomes its opposite.
- **[hypothesis]** That a month in another way of life yields real J rather than a tourist impression. **Plausible but unmeasured.** Tested on the long trace (M9): are grown people who had exchanges less rigid, better able to hold a boundary? And it can be tested **causally**: randomize exchanges within a node, with consent (`08_DATA` §6). **We are obliged to test it.**

---

## 4. Why there are exactly two

The temptation is to add a third, a fifth, a tenth: diligence, honesty, respect for elders, love of learning. Each is admirable on its own.

**It cannot be done.** Every added virtue is **content**, and content is forbidden to the network (`03_SOVEREIGNTY`): it requires an interpreter, the interpreter requires a center, and the center kills evolution.

These two are **an exception, justified not by being good but by being structural:**

- they **do not say what to be** — they protect the **capacity to be anything at all**;
- they cover **both** deaths of the boundary, and nothing beyond;
- each is a **ritual, not a norm**: the child **plays** and **travels**; he does not memorize.

**Diligence, honesty, faith, discipline — these are the node's faith.** Let every node raise its children as it sees fit. The network proposes only what **the child will need in order to choose for himself later.**

---

## 5. Honest limits

- **[hypothesis]** That the erasure game will interest a child and hold as a daily ritual. **A bet.** If it fails, sovereignty and data validity collapse together.
- **[hypothesis]** That a month in another way of life yields real J rather than a tourist impression. Plausible but **unmeasured.** It will have to be measured on the long trace (M9): are grown people who had exchanges less rigid? Testable — and we are obliged to test it.
- **[risk] The exchange may traumatize.** A month away from the attachment apex, for a small child, is **a violation of M1**, not a virtue. Therefore: **the exchange begins no earlier than the child is ready for it**, and in early childhood **the apex travels with the child.** The J virtue is **never bought at the price of the I axiom.** This is not a compromise but an order of priority: first there must be someone to let the world in, then the world is let in.
- **[risk] The virtues will degenerate into obligation.** A ritual turned into compulsion is already a collapse into the pole of order — that is, **precisely the pathology the virtues protect against.** The sign of degeneration: the child **does not want** to play at erasing and **does not want** to travel. Then the node has broken the ritual and must admit it.

---

**The formula of this file.** We do not teach a child to be good — we do not know what that means, and we have no right to decide it for him. We give him **two habits, without which he will not be able to decide for himself:** to dispose, every day, of who he remains, and to see, every year, that one can be otherwise. The first keeps him from dissolving. The second keeps him from freezing. Everything else is his business, and his node's, and evolution's.
