# 05. ATTACHMENT

### The single substantive axiom — and why exactly this one

---

## 0. What is asserted

**M1: every child has a permanent attachment figure, designated before the child appears, not detachable at night or in early childhood.**

A specific human being — not "the collective," not "the staff," not "the shift" — whom the child goes to when things are bad, and who is with the child at night.

This is the **only** substantive rule in the whole protocol that a node may neither reinterpret nor break while remaining in the network. Everything else in upbringing is given over to the node's faith (`03_SOVEREIGNTY`) and to evolution (`04_EVOLUTION`). This file answers two questions: why an exception exists at all — and why there is exactly one.

---

## 1. Why the exception exists: irreversibility, not morality

The project on principle introduces no substantive rules — they create a center, a center is a monopoly, monopoly kills evolution. The one exception is justified not by "attachment is good" but by a strict criterion:

**A question may be handed to evolution when the price of error is paid by one who entered it voluntarily. It may not — when the price is paid by one who did not choose, and paid irreversibly.**

An adult in a node chose (M8 — free entry and exit). A child did not. And damage to early attachment is **irreversible**: it is not repaired by later good treatment, because it is laid down in the period when the very capacity for close relationships is formed.

Evolution *would* find the attachment optimum — but it would find it **too dearly**: by trying out the nodes that break that attachment, and paying for the search with generations of broken people. Selection works here (a bad node ultimately loses on M9), but it works through **irreversible harm to one who did not choose.** At exactly this point the speed of selection yields to irreversibility, and the architect is obliged to pull the variant out of the search in advance.

**One irreversibility — one exception.** This is not "the first of the values"; it is the only one, and it stands here not because it is the most important in content but because it is the only one where the price of error is indelible and falls on one who did not consent.

---

## 2. The empirical grounding: what is known about early attachment

The axiom rests not on ideology but on the corpus of developmental data.

- **Monotropy (Bowlby).** A child has a hierarchy of attachment figures with one principal apex; the presence of a permanent apex is not a luxury but a condition of normal formation of emotional regulation.
- **Institutional deprivation.** Classic data (Spitz; later, the Romanian orphans of Ceaușescu, the ERA study) show: children who received care but **without a permanent figure** display persistent disturbances — from developmental delays to an inability to form attachment, some of which is not compensated even by later adoption into good families. Irreversibility here is not theory but a measured fact.
- **The sensitive period.** The capacity for attachment forms in an early window; what is missed in it is made up poorly or not at all — unlike almost everything else a child will catch up on.

*Status:* [fact] — that a permanent figure is necessary and that its absence yields severe and partly irreversible consequences. [hypothesis] — the exact bounds of "early childhood" and "non-detachability at night" as engineering parameters; these are refinable, but the principle itself follows firmly from the data.

---

## 3. The kibbutzim: why the axiom is worded specifically about the night

The largest experiment in collective child-rearing in history — the Israeli kibbutzim — tested exactly this boundary, and the result directly set the wording of M1.

**What they did.** For the liberation of woman and the building of a new collective human being, children were raised in *children's houses* (*beit yeladim*): shared living, shared care, professional caregivers — and **communal night sleep, apart from the parents.** The child spent the day in the collective and **slept not with the mother.**

**What worked.** The daytime collective, multiple care, early socialization — along these axes kibbutz children developed normally or surpassed their peers. This is direct evidence for the project: **distributed daytime care (allo-parenting) does no harm** — it is the species norm (`07_ECONOMY`, Hrdy).

**What broke — and this is the key.** Precisely the **communal night sleep.** Attachment research in the kibbutzim (Sagi, Aviezer, Van IJzendoorn and colleagues) showed an elevated share of insecure attachment among communal-sleeping children compared with those who slept at home — all else equal within the same system. The night, separation in the dark, the absence of a permanent figure at the moment of greatest vulnerability — that is what produced the effect.

**How it ended.** The kibbutzim **themselves** abandoned communal night sleep and returned children to the family for the night — and the movement for this was led largely by **the mothers themselves.** A system built on communal sleep dismantled precisely that, keeping the collective day.

**The conclusion, cast directly into M1:** it is not the collective that breaks a child — it is the **nighttime severance from the permanent figure.** Therefore the axiom prohibits not collective upbringing (that is sovereign and welcomed), but exactly one thing: **the severance of the child from its permanent apex at night and in early childhood.** M1 is the learned lesson of the kibbutzim, not an abstract ideal.

*A note on status.* The interpretation of the kibbutz data is debatable: some researchers attribute the effect not to the sleep itself but to the quality of night supervision or to cultural factors. But even a cautious reading leaves the conclusion standing: where a permanent figure was absent at night, attachment suffered. The project takes the minimal, indisputable part of the lesson.

---

## 4. What the axiom does NOT prohibit

It is important to delimit precisely, because the temptation to read M1 expansively is great:

- **Does not prohibit collective upbringing.** A day in the collective, many caregivers, a nursery, a commune — are sovereign and welcomed.
- **Does not prohibit allo-parenting.** On the contrary: distributed care is a load-bearing idea of the project (`07_ECONOMY`). Many hands are the species norm.
- **Does not require the apex to be the biological mother.** The permanent figure may be the father, an adoptive parent, a designated caregiver, **a grandmother or grandfather** — **any specific unchanging person.** Permanence and non-detachability at night are required, not blood kinship. **The apex is a functional slot, not a kinship slot: whoever sleeps with the child is the apex.** A grandmother who is with the child at night does not "stand in for the mother" — she **is** the mother in the only sense M1 recognizes.
- **Does not prohibit surrogacy or role division** (`07_ECONOMY`). Who gestated and who became the attachment apex may be different people. The axiom is about the permanence of the figure *after* the child appears, not about the manner of conception or gestation.
- **Does not dictate one family model.** A single parent, a same-sex couple, a plural configuration — any, if it contains a permanent apex not detachable at night.

The axiom is narrow **deliberately**: it excises exactly one irreversible error and touches nothing beyond it. Everything that is not "nighttime severance from the permanent figure in early childhood" is sovereign.

---

## 4-bis. The apex is mortal — and this is the commonest way M1 breaks

When discussing violations of M1 it is easy to think of malice: a conveyor-belt node, rotating night staff, "the children are everyone's." But **in the real world attachment is most often severed not by evil but by death.**

The apex dies. A seventy-eight-year-old grandfather who became the apex will sever the child's attachment at age ten — not out of malice but **by the actuarial table.** And that is exactly the irreversibility M1 exists to prevent. The death of an apex strikes the child as hard as its absence.

**Hence the mandatory successor apex:**

- **the successor is designated in advance** and **is already present in the child's life.** A stranger who appears after the funeral is not a successor but a **second loss**;
- in choosing an apex, its **expected persistence** across early childhood and beyond is taken into account. This is an **actuarial requirement, not an age bar**: a sixty-year-old grandmother is a perfectly good apex; a seventy-eight-year-old grandfather is a poor bet, and that is arithmetic, not disrespect for old age;
- this is **M12 (the node's will) applied one level down — to the person.** The node names the node's successors; the apex names the apex's successor.

This also dissolves the apparent conflict with the market of elderly customers (`07_ECONOMY` §2): **the grandfather buys continuation but does not become the apex** — not because he is a "deficient parent" but because he will not outlast the term. The role evolution gave the old is that of **allo-parent, not apex** (the grandmother hypothesis). He is added. The apex is supplied by the node.

---

## 5. How this is kept without a center

The axiom is substantive, but enforcement is the same as for the whole protocol: **eucharistic communion** (`02_COMMUNION`), not a supervisory body.

- A node practicing nighttime severance from the permanent figure (communal dormitories without an unchanging apex, rotating night staff as the primary model) is **visible** through the open method (M10).
- Nodes that read this as a violation of M1 **break communion with it**: it loses data, insurance, recognition.
- The child is harmed *now*, while the sanction works *slowly* — an honestly named hole (`02_COMMUNION` §8.4). The partial answer is that the violation is visible at once (the method is open), and that the state remains a subject of child protection in its jurisdiction.

M1 is not something anyone audits with an inspection. It is a line whose crossing nodes **see** (through M10) and to which they **respond** (through rupture). As with everything in the network: not supervision, but distributed reaction.

---

**The formula of this file.** The project surrenders everything to evolution — except one line, beyond which the price is paid by one who did not choose, and paid irreversibly. That line is not "attachment in general" but the concrete lesson of the kibbutzim: **do not sever a child from its permanent figure at night and in early childhood.** One irreversibility — one axiom. Beyond it: freedom of faith and the work of selection.
