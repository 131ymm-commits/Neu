# 01. THE PROTOCOL

**Status:** specification, version 1.3. Forking is legitimate.
**Read as:** an RFC, not a charter. There are no values here. There are conditions of connectivity.

---

## 0. The distinction that governs everything

**A protocol is not a law. A protocol is a condition of connectivity.**

A machine that does not speak TCP/IP is not forbidden and not prosecuted. It is simply not on the internet. Nobody hunts it, nobody tries it, no enforcement body exists — and precisely for that reason the internet cannot be captured.

The same here. An organization that does not follow the protocol is **not a violator**. It is simply **not a node of Foundation**. It may exist, raise children, make money — the network neither hinders it nor could. It only will not receive what the network gives: data exchange, AI guidance, insurance, recognition.

Two conclusions follow, and they must be absorbed before anything else:

1. **We forbid nothing.** We describe what it means to be in the network.
2. **We control no one.** No supervisory body exists or will exist — it would be a center, a center is a monopoly, and monopoly kills evolution (see `04_EVOLUTION`).

---

## 1. Terminology

- **Node** — an independent organization that bears and raises children and follows this protocol. Legal form, country, internal structure: the node's own business.
- **Network** — the set of nodes in communion with one another. The network has no legal personality, no governing body, no registry, no owner.
- **Communion** — a state of mutual recognition between two nodes (see `02_COMMUNION`).
- **Breaking communion** — a unilateral, public act of non-recognition. The only sanction that exists in the network.
- **Fork** — a group of nodes splitting off with its own reading of the protocol (or its own protocol). A legitimate outcome.
- **The node's faith** — all content: method, way of life, ideology, regime, aesthetics, religion, staff selection. Sovereign.
- **The node's land** — money, premises, jurisdiction, people. Sovereign.

## 2. Node obligations (MUST)

Twelve. Everything not listed here is sovereign.

### M1. The attachment axiom

**Every child has a permanent attachment figure, designated before the child appears, not detachable at night or in early childhood.**

Not "the collective," not "the staff," not "the shift" — a specific human being whom the child goes to when things are bad, and who is with the child at night.

**The apex is a functional slot, not a kinship slot.** Whoever sleeps with the child *is* the apex. Mother, father, grandmother, grandfather, adoptive parent, designated caregiver — the protocol is indifferent to blood, title and generation. A grandmother who is with the child at night **does not "replace the mother" — she *is* the mother** in the only sense M1 recognizes. One thing matters: **who is there at night and does not change.**

**The apex is not a job.** It cannot be an employee: an employee is fired, an employee quits — and M1 breaks with every personnel decision. Therefore:

> **The apex leaves only together with the child.**

She cannot be dismissed (that is a severance of attachment) and cannot walk away (leaving means taking the child). This is precisely how a parent works. A node that wants a non-kin apex takes a person **for eighteen years and on that condition** — otherwise it has no apex but staff, and M1 is not satisfied. This also settles the question for corporate nodes (`07_ECONOMY` §2-bis).

**If the apex harms the child, the criminal law of the country applies, not the network.** M1 protects **the continuity of the apex, not the impunity of a particular person**: if she harms, the state removes her and the successor steps in (below). The network builds no parallel justice system and does not try (`02_COMMUNION` §8.4).

**A successor apex (mandatory).** The most common way M1 breaks in the real world is that **the apex dies.** A seventy-eight-year-old grandfather who became the apex will sever the child's attachment at age ten — not out of malice but by the actuarial table. Therefore:
- every apex has a **designated successor**, and that successor is **already present in the child's life.** A stranger who appears after the funeral is not a successor but a second loss;
- in choosing an apex, its **expected persistence** across early childhood and beyond is taken into account. This is an actuarial requirement, not an age bar;
- this is M12 (the node's will) applied one level down — to the person.

The only substantive rule in the protocol. Not even a fully sovereign node may break it. Grounding: `05_ATTACHMENT`. In short — this is not morality but **irreversibility**. Selection will punish a node that violates the axiom, but it will punish **with people already broken**, and they pay the price of an experiment they did not choose. One irreversibility in the system — one exception to "we don't interfere."

Everything else in child-rearing: as you please.

### M2. Three prohibitions — without definitions

**Prohibited:**
- **violence against children;**
- **drugs;**
- **propaganda of violence.**

**There will be no definitions. This is not an oversight — it is the design.**

Why: a definition requires an **interpreter**, an interpreter requires a **court**, a court requires a **center**, a center is a **monopoly**, and monopoly **kills evolution**. By writing a definition we would build with our own hands the one thing we swore not to build.

And the definition is impossible anyway. Try drawing the line on "drugs": caffeine — yes? sport (endorphins under load) — yes? spicy food — which, strictly speaking, is not a taste but a **burn of TRPV1 pain receptors and an endorphin response to pain**, pharmacologically closer to a drug than much that is permitted. Any line drawn will be arbitrary, and its interpretation will become the ground of capture.

**And there is a deeper grounding — it is in `06_BOUNDARY`, and it is decisive: there is nothing to define.**

Harm does not sit in the substance. Harm is the **collapse of the boundary into a pole**: either into order (obsession, addiction, dogma — one mode crowds out all others, the world stops entering) or into chaos (dissolution — nothing is retained, no form lasts). Anything at all is harmless at the boundary and lethal in a pole: coffee, sport, spice, food, sleep, mathematics, prayer. A list of substances is **categorically the wrong kind of answer**, because pathology is a **relation**, not a thing.

**And there is no measure either — this is proven.** The boundary μ_sc is not empirically detectable by any single method: independent criticality detectors contradict one another on the same data (`06_BOUNDARY` §4). Therefore **a central court on harm is impossible not ideologically but mathematically: there is nothing to judge with.** Distributed judgment is not a choice but the only possibility.

**Therefore:**
- the prohibition is **general and binding**;
- **interpretation belongs to the node**;
- disagreement with another's interpretation is resolved **not by a court but by breaking communion** (`02_COMMUNION`);
- **a criterion instead of a definition:** not "what is being done to the child," but **"is the child's boundary being preserved"** — the capacity to hold oneself (I) and to let the world in (Δτ·J). Violence = the forced collapse of a boundary by another person. A drug = anything that collapses the boundary, whatever it may be. Propaganda of violence = teaching that collapsing another's boundary is the norm. This is a **language of judgment, not an instrument** (`06_BOUNDARY` §5).

Node A holds that a belting is violence; node B holds that it is discipline. No one will arbitrate. A breaks communion with B and says why, publicly. The other nodes read and decide for themselves. The interpretation that keeps communion with the majority survives; the interpretation that repels everyone dies with its node. **This is not the absence of enforcement. This is distributed enforcement.**

### M3. Public communion list

**A node must publish and maintain a list of the nodes it is in communion with, and must announce ruptures publicly, with the reason stated.**

The list is the only mandatory reporting in the network. From these lists anyone builds the graph. **There is no registry; there is a graph.** No one owns it; anyone can read it.

### M4. Data exchange

**A node must contribute de-identified data on child development and on the results of its methods to the common pool — and has the right to draw from it.**

Data is the **currency of communion** and the network's principal product (`08_DATA`). It feeds the AI that tells nodes what works. A node that leaves the exchange loses the AI advantage and gradually loses the competition — mechanically, without any sanction.

Format, depth and privacy boundaries: `08_DATA` and `15_MIRROR`. One red line: **a child's data belongs to the child**; only numbers leave the node, never raw material.

**Why self-reporting is not enough.** A node reports on itself — so it has a direct incentive to flatter itself. A dataset of self-reports is worthless, and with it the network's main function collapses. Therefore the load-bearing part of the exchange is **machine-collected data** (the Markov Mirror, `15_MIRROR`): what comes off the instrument, not out of the press release, is hard to fake. **The Mirror is not merely an aid to the child; it is the audit substrate without which data exchange means nothing.**

### M5. Participation in insurance (the catch)

**A node must accept the children of a falling node with which it was in communion — and may expect the same for its own.**

Nodes fall: money runs out, the state arrives, a scandal breaks. Children must not fall with them. This is the only mechanism that answers the honest objection "the child pays for a failed experiment" (`14_RESILIENCE`).

An important consequence: **breaking communion also breaks insurance.** Which is why nobody breaks it on a whim.

### M6. Self-funding

**A node must fund its own existence.**

No money, no node. This is not cruelty but physics: a node living off a donor (state, patron, foundation) has a **hidden center** above it, and a center is a monopoly and a point of capture. The donor turns off the tap — the node is dead, and all its "independence" was paper.

How to earn is sovereign (`07_ECONOMY`). Surrogacy, services, education, data, anything.

**But: no single source may dominate a node's revenue.** A node living on the money of one payer — a corporation, a state, a patron — **has that payer above it as a hidden center.** On the day the payer says "change the upbringing or we withdraw," the node will have no choice, and all its sovereignty will prove to be paper. Concentration of money is concentration of power — the same logic as in genetic antitrust (M7). One payer's share of a node's revenue is capped; a node that exceeds it **has in fact ceased to be independent**, and this is visible from outside.

### M7. Anti-monopoly — including genetic

**No participant may gain control of the network. Not through charter, not through money, not through the genome.**

This is the **one principle not surrendered to evolution** — because selection by itself tends toward concentration: the winner eats the market, the successful absorb their neighbours, and in the limit one devours all. Free selection without an antitrust rule **produces monopoly** — that is not a bug, it is its attractor. The internet is uncaptured not because it evolved, but because distribution was wired in **before** selection and protected **from** it.

Specifically forbidden:
- a single center, registry, body, off switch;
- absorption of nodes by a node up to dominance in the graph;
- **genetic monopoly**: one donor's share in the population of a node or region may not exceed the ceiling set by the network.

**On genetic antitrust — plainly, because it is not obvious.** A wealthy donor who wants tens of thousands of children is a legitimate and welcome participant (`07_ECONOMY`, launch model). But a hundred thousand half-siblings in one population is:
- a **clonal colony**: one genome = one lock; a parasite that finds the key cuts down all of them at once (precisely the fragility of monoculture the network is built against);
- **inbreeding**: half-siblings will meet without knowing it (the same reason ordinary sperm banks impose per-donor limits — mechanics, not morality);
- **capture**: whoever owns the gene pool owns the network.

**The mechanism is not a ceiling but a progressive price with feedback.** No prohibition is needed; market physics is:

1. The price of a child from donor D in population P **rises superlinearly** with D's existing share in that population. The first child at base price. The thousandth at a multiple.
2. **The surcharge above base price does not stay with the node.** It goes into a **diversity fund** that subsidizes children from under-represented genomes in **the same** population.
3. Result: **each additional child from a dominant donor directly finances children from his competitors.** His own money buys his counterweight.

Why this and not a ceiling: **on a billionaire a ceiling is a prohibition, and a price is no obstacle.** A wealthy donor will pay any price. But if his own surcharge automatically funds rival genomes, his share **physically cannot run away** — it damps itself the harder the faster it grows. The rule is self-correcting; it requires no enforcement and no center.

A donor funds as much as he wishes and receives as many children as he wishes. No skew arises. This is antitrust, not morality: the money arrives, the monopoly does not form, the axiom holds.

The price curve and fund parameters are empirical, refined by the network. Shares are tracked by a **distributed parentage registry** (hashed donor identifiers and counters published by nodes; anyone can compute a share, no one owns the registry — see `07_ECONOMY`). A node that does not apply the progression to itself loses communion.

### M8. Free entry and exit

**An adult enters and leaves freely, without punishment and without loss beyond the natural.**

A node closed on exit is a cult, and it drops out of the network at once: **the impossibility of leaving destroys selection**, and without selection there is no evolution. Freedom of exit is not humanism but a working condition of the engine: only the people who leave can tell a node that it is dead.

The child is a special case: covered by M5 (insurance) and M1 (attachment); the child's own right over his data is `08_DATA`.

### The transaction test — where the stake is real

M8 extends to all participants in reproduction. But **proportionately.**

**The gamete donor:** consent is required, revocable up to fertilization. **That is all.** This is a five-minute act, not a kidney and not a pregnancy; building an apparatus of anti-coercion around it is an error of proportion. Paying for gametes is permitted and right.

**The gestating woman:** here the stake is real — nine months, the body, medical risk. Here the test applies:

> **Is the person worse off if she refuses?**
> **No worse → an offer → there is a transaction.**
> **Worse → a threat → there is no transaction, there is extraction.**

**Money is not the problem but the solution:** it was invented so that **"no" became possible.** Before money, value was taken; with money it is offered. Refusing to pay does not make the deal cleaner — it makes it feudal (`07_ECONOMY` §1). What is inadmissible is not payment but **the absence of alternatives**: without them, payment is a fig leaf.

**Consent may be bought. Consent may not be extorted.**

### M9. The long trace: the only true selection signal

**A node must publish aggregate long-term outcomes of its graduates — at 20, 30, 40 years.**

Why: evolution requires a **fitness signal**. Today a node is selected on money, clients, reputation and data volume — but all of these are **proxies**, while the real outcome arrives a quarter of a century later. This is Goodhart's law with a twenty-year lag: the network will optimize for what is measurable today and drift from what it was built for.

Therefore:
- **the grown children report themselves**, voluntarily — they are the only ones who know the truth;
- the node aggregates and publishes; comparison between nodes becomes possible;
- no one judges anyone — the data is simply public.

**A node's fitness is reported by the people it raised.**

**What exactly to measure — and why Goodhart dies here.** Not income, not "happiness," not success: nodes will optimize any proxy instead of the human being. Measure the **integrity of the boundary** (`06_BOUNDARY`): can the grown person hold form and let the world in — has he not collapsed into addiction and rigidity, has he not dissolved into an inability to hold anything at all.

And the keystone: **the capacity to raise a child is itself the direct test of the boundary.** A child is the longest form a human can hold: twenty years of persistence while daily letting the world in. One collapsed into order will not let the child in; one collapsed into chaos will not hold him. **The project's goal and its metric coincide.** There is no proxy — nothing to hack.

**And a graduate's refusal to speak is also a measurement** (`13_GRADUATE` §3): they scattered and never looked back — the node did not work.

### M10. The method is open

**A node must publish its method — what it does and how.** Not the children's data (that belongs to the child, `08_DATA`), but the **method**: the way of life, regime, pedagogy, structure of care, staff selection criteria.

Two arguments, both mechanical.

**Heredity.** Evolution = variation + selection + **copying**. Without the third, a successful variant dies with its node, the gains are lost, and this is no longer evolution but a graveyard with turnover.

**Anti-monopoly through the back door.** Secret know-how is a **moat**, and a moat leads to monopoly. This bypasses M7 not through the charter and not through the genome, but through know-how. The door closes here.

*Honest counter-argument:* an open method means no moat — so why invest? Answer: restaurants publish recipes and still differ enormously — competition runs on **execution**, not on secrets.

### M11. No one owns a human being

**The grown person leaves with zero debt.** He "works off" nothing, owes the node nothing, disposes of his own data, and has the right to testify publicly about the node — **including against it.**

This is not ethics. This is **M7 applied one level down**: a node that binds its graduate with an obligation has **captured** him. A monopoly on a human being is no better than a monopoly on a registry or a genome.

**And it is metrology, not humanism** (`13_GRADUATE` §2): a bound graduate will report what he is supposed to report, and his words are worth nothing. **Only a free graduate yields a valid signal** — and M9 rests on that.

Structural consequence: **you cannot leave a cult; you leave a node owing nothing.** This is the testable difference between a node and a cult — not a declaration but a fact visible from outside.

### M12. The node's will (the catch trigger)

**A node must name, in advance and publicly, the nodes in communion that will take its children if it falls.**

Without this, M5 is a wish, not a mechanism: there is no center, no one to declare a node fallen, and "the children will be caught" remains a phrase that means nothing at three in the morning when the state seals the building.

- The will is public and is updated together with the communion list (M3).
- No will, or the named nodes are gone — the **graph** decides: those who were in communion with the fallen node take them, by proximity in the graph.
- **Breaking communion removes a node from others' wills** — one more reason a rupture is costly to both sides. **But the obligation to receive a fallen node's children survives the rupture** (`14_RESILIENCE` §4).
- **The will must be pre-executed**, not a document: named nodes, named people, agreed places and **money in reserve** — a node must be able to pay for its own funeral (`14_RESILIENCE` §6).

## 3. Who may found a node

**Anyone. No permission is required — because there is no one to grant it.**

There is no admissions committee, no certification, no registry of nodes (`02_COMMUNION` §4). **A node becomes a node by doing the thing and publishing its communion list (M3).** Other nodes then decide whether to recognize it.

> **Entry is not granted. Entry is recognized — or it is not.**

A node may be founded by: **a corporation, a family or clan, a city, a state, a university, a monastery, a trade union, a co-operative, a diaspora — or a two-hundred-person factory that simply decided to.** There is no one to forbid them: there is no center.

**The form is likewise free.** Separate campuses, dormitories, company towns, city blocks, scattered flats, a village. The only constraint is **M1, and it concerns the night, not the building**: the child sleeps with his apex. A campus where families and grandmothers live, with a shared kitchen, a shared school and shared daytime care, where at night the child is with his apex, is **a kibbutz minus the children's house** — that is, a kibbutz with its single error corrected (`05_ATTACHMENT` §3).

**And this is not indulgence but a requirement of method.** The first generation of nodes is not selected — it is appointed, and the first real signal arrives in twenty years (`08_DATA` §8). The only hedge against a blind start is **maximal variance**: many small, cheap, different, expendable nodes — not three richly funded sects. **"Let a thousand flowers bloom" is not a slogan but a working condition of selection.**

## 4. Node sovereignty (MAY): everything else

**Whose the land, his the faith.**

The node decides for itself, without anyone's approval:

- **how to bear children** — naturally, IVF, surrogacy (legitimate everywhere and without reservation, `07_ECONOMY`), donation, any form;
- **who bears, and for whom** — family composition, singles, couples of any composition, donors, commissioning parents;
- **how to raise** — method, regime, discipline, education, religion, ideology, aesthetics, language;
- **what to believe** — secular, religious, techno-, national, any nodes. All within their right;
- **whom to admit** — selection of staff, participants, children;
- **how to earn** — any model legal in its jurisdiction;
- **what data** to collect beyond the mandatory minimum;
- **how to interpret** the three prohibitions of M2.

The network **holds no opinion** on any of these. This is not tolerance — it is architecture: a network with an opinion on content acquires a center to hold that opinion.

In detail: `03_SOVEREIGNTY`.

---

## 5. What the network never does (MUST NOT for the network)

- **Holds no center.** No body, no office, no council, no registry, no off switch.
- **Does not judge.** There is not and cannot be an instance establishing the fact of a violation. There are only nodes deciding whom they are in communion with.
- **Does not define content.** No canon of upbringing, no approved methods, no "correct human being."
- **Owns nothing.** Not the data, not the nodes, not the children, not the protocol. The protocol is public domain; forking is legitimate.
- **Does not moralize and does not apologize.** You cannot please everyone. There will be scandals, intrigue and investigations — a sign of life, not of failure (`17_RISKS`).

---

## 6. Enforcement

One mechanism only — **eucharistic communion**: mutual recognition and its rupture. Fully described in `02_COMMUNION`.

Briefly: a node that violates the protocol is punished by no one. People simply stop being in communion with it. It loses data, AI guidance, insurance and reputation — and dies economically, because others stopped dealing with it. Exactly like a spammer on the internet: he is not arrested, he is not accepted.

---

## 7. Versioning and forking

This protocol is version **1.3**. It **must** change.

Forking is legitimate and expected: a group of nodes that disagrees may declare its own version and build its own graph. Schism is not a catastrophe but the way the system tries variants.

**The one thing that cannot be forked without leaving the network:** M7 (anti-monopoly). A fork that repeals anti-monopoly builds a center — that is, it builds something other than Foundation, whatever it calls itself.
