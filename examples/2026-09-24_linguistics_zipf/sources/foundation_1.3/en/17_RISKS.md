# 17. RISKS

### Pre-registration of failures

---

## 0. The rule of this file

**Everything that can fail is named here IN ADVANCE — before it has failed.**

This is the discipline of pre-registration: the hypothesis and the way to refute it are declared **before** the experiment, not after. A project that names its risks **after** they have materialized is not honest — it has been caught.

**Each risk has a trigger sign.** If the sign appears — **the risk has materialized, and that must be said aloud**, not explained away.

---

## 1. RED: they kill the project or break children

### R1. All the nodes are wrong together

**What.** The nodes are self-selected, read one protocol, share one picture of the world. **A common blind spot by construction.** The communion graph becomes collective cover-up with a mathematical justification.

**Why it is not solved.** Enforcement is nodes checking each other. Checkers with a common blindness see nothing.

**What mitigates.** The mandated difference of the first nodes; the free tier (ordinary families outside the network); the state (outside the network); **the graduates — the only actor able to testify against all of them at once.** But they will speak **in twenty-five years.**

**Precedent.** The internet shared a blind spot in security (TCP/IP without authentication) for thirty years — and **survived, because the correction came from outside, through damage, without permission from within.**

**The rule for that period:** **build nothing irreversible inside the window.**

**Trigger sign:** nodes stop diverging in judgment; ruptures of communion cease; criticism comes only from outside the network.

---

### R2. The first cohort pays for our ignorance

**What.** The first children grow up in a system that **knows nothing yet.** We cannot check whether what we do works — the data does not exist **by construction**; it will exist in twenty years.

**Not solved.** It is the price of ever knowing anything. Children of **every** generation have paid it — it simply was not written into the charter before.

**What we are obliged to do:** observe, do not experiment; interventions only with consent, and minimal; **minimal ambition — not "raise a new human being" but do not break an ordinary one**; three nodes from day one.

**Trigger sign:** the language of ambition ("a new human being," "a breakthrough," "an evolutionary leap") appears in the project's texts in place of the language of caution.

---

### R3. Payment does not save a woman who has no choice

**What.** Capitalism pays fairly **only where the seller has alternatives.** Commercial surrogacy in a desperate population is **feudalism that pays.**

**Why it is dangerous.** The market will press toward **cheap jurisdictions** — that is, toward where women have the fewest alternatives. This is an **economic gradient, not anyone's malice**, and therefore stronger than any declaration.

**The test** ("is she worse off if she refuses?") is right, but **hard to verify.**

**Trigger sign:** nodes systematically choose low-income jurisdictions with weak protection; the long trace of gestating women (M9) is not collected, or shows deterioration.

---

### R4. A node degenerates into a cult and the network learns too late

**What.** An individual node closes in, acquires an enemy in its norm, makes exit expensive.

**What works.** Rupture of communion; the catch of the children (`14_RESILIENCE` §4 — **the obligation to receive them survives the rupture**); the open method; the exchange of children (every year the child lives outside).

**The limit.** **Rupture works over months. The child is in pain today.** Here only the state and the criminal law work. **The network is not the police.**

**Trigger sign:** a stable word for "outsiders" appears in the node's language; leaving becomes expensive; child exchanges cease; the erasure rate falls to zero.

---

### R5. Capture by a state

**What.** The state takes the children, closes the node, criminalizes the participants. **The Syrian case at SOS: children were taken from families under regime pressure and later returned to the authorities with no follow-up on their fate.**

**Not solved.** Jurisdictional diversity saves **the network**, not **those** children. M5 and M12 work **only if the state releases them.**

**Mitigation.** Do not build in weak states (`12_JURISDICTION` §7 — choosing a weak jurisdiction is itself a red flag); the child's ties abroad; sometimes a second citizenship.

**Trigger sign:** nodes relocate to where no one checks on them.

---

### R6. Extraterritorial criminalization

**What.** Italy, October 2024: surrogacy declared a **"universal crime"** — an Italian is prosecuted **at home** for surrogacy **anywhere in the world.** If the model spreads, **participants become criminals at home** regardless of where the node operates.

**Not solved.** Only by having no nodes dependent on one country.

**The trend is against us.** Italy, Kenya, Slovakia (into the constitution), Spain, Greece; **the Hague convention collapsed in March 2026 — there will be no harmonization.**

**Trigger sign:** a second country passes an Italian-style law.

## 2. AMBER: they cripple the project without killing it

### R7. The recommender's blind startup window

The long trace takes a year or more; a bribe pays within a quarter. **In the early years there is no trace — the system is at its most corruptible when it is youngest.**
**Mitigation:** start in **fast domains** (consumables — repurchase gives a signal within weeks); **stay out of slow ones** (school, house, doctor, pension) until a trace exists.
**Sign:** the recommender enters expensive domains before a trace has accumulated.

### R8. The index becomes a monopoly

One index = one center = death. **Running an index must be cheap and forkable.** An index costing a billion is an industry, and industries get bought.
**Sign:** fewer than three indexes; standing one up is expensive.

### R9. A bribe that does no harm

A seller pays, the index promotes him, **but his product is genuinely good.** Satisfaction does not fall — nothing is detected.
**Mitigation:** a distributional audit (publish the distribution of recommendations).
**Sign:** one seller receives a share of recommendations disproportionate to his share of good products.

### R10. A single AI through the back door

Federated learning, a shared "base model," a convenient API — **and the network acquires a live center**, bypassing M7 through the model.
**Sign:** a shared live model appears that someone updates and everyone depends on.

### R11. The node's reserve is fiction

"Another node will take you" = "another will pay." Nodes will **understate their reserves**, and adequacy is hard to verify in advance.
**Sign:** a node falls — and it turns out there is nothing to pay for the evacuation.

### R12. The exchange degenerates into elite tourism

Rich nodes exchange with rich ones and it becomes a finishing school. **It kills all four functions of the exchange at once** — J, anti-capture, bridges, the standby pool of apexes.
**Sign:** the graph distance of exchanges falls; the difference of faith between sender and host tends to zero.

### R13. Genetic skew at the start

**The rich will fund this disproportionately**, and their genomes will be over-represented. The progressive price is the only answer, and it has **never been tested.**
**Sign:** the top ten donors' share of a node's population exceeds the threshold, and the diversity fund fails to damp the growth.

### R14. The record is seized

The child cannot erase what has already been copied: by a state, an attacker, a court. The common pool yields **numbers, not lives** (conditional independence is checkable) — **but his personal device can be seized.**
**Sign:** the first seizure. It will happen.

## 3. GREY: untested bets, not risks

These are **not** dangers. They are **hypotheses that may not hold** — and then the project honestly declares a negative result.

| Bet | If it fails |
|---|---|
| **The high-fertility mechanism is separable from the subjugation of women** | The project's central bet. If not separable — the project is wrong at its foundation. |
| **Men will take half the direct care** | There is no historical precedent. If they do not — "distributed load" turns out to be a woman's load spread over several women. |
| **The erasure game holds as a daily ritual** | If it does not — the child's sovereignty and the dataset's validity collapse **together.** |
| **A month in another way of life yields real J** | If not — the second virtue is decorative, and anti-capture with it. |
| **Graduates will want to stay connected** | If they do not — **that is the answer** (`13_GRADUATE` §3). |
| **An honest recommender beats a bought one** | Bought advertising has a thousandfold budget. If it loses — the project's economy collapses. |
| **Exchange families will step forward in the catastrophic case** | If they do not — the catastrophic case has no answer. |
| **A transferable law of development exists at all** | **It may not.** Then the project's result is **the proof that there is no law.** That too is knowledge. |

## 4. Signs that the project has become what it swore not to be

**Read this list once a year. Aloud.**

- [ ] **The network has acquired a face.** A **founder figure** has appeared in the press, at conferences, in the texts (`11_START` §6). — *Everything will now proceed on the SOS timetable.*
- [ ] **A body has appeared.** A council, a committee, a certification, a registry, a "standards working group."
- [ ] **A shared live AI model has appeared.**
- [ ] **There are now more than twelve obligations.** Every superfluous "must not" is **somebody's capture dressed up as care** (`16_OBJECTIONS` §13).
- [ ] **"Outsiders" have entered the language.**
- [ ] **Leaving has become expensive** — for an adult, for a child, for a node.
- [ ] **Ruptures of communion have ceased.** Everyone agrees with everyone. **A complete graph is a crystal — that is, a de facto monopoly.**
- [ ] **The long trace is published selectively.** Angry graduates have been scrubbed. — *This is literally SOS with the buried 2020 report.*
- [ ] **The erasure rate has fallen to zero.** — *Were you leaning on them?*
- [ ] **Failure has stopped being survivable for a founder.** — *Then failures will start being hidden.*

**Any single tick is not "food for thought." It is a failure that has already occurred.**

## 5. What we have not foreseen

**This is the most important section, and it is empty by construction.**

For thirty years the internet did not see that it had no security. For half a century the kibbutzim did not see what the night was breaking. For seventy-five years SOS did not see that closedness kills.

**All of them were certain their risk list was complete.**

> **Our list is incomplete. We do not know where.**

The only defence is **to make it cheap for whoever sees it to say so:** a graduate with zero debt and the right to publish everything; a family that left without loss; a node that split off with a fork; a critic who is read rather than fought off; a state that is not in the network.

**Correction comes from those the system harmed. Always. So their voice is not a threat to the project. It is its only chance.**

---

**The formula of this file.** We wrote down our failures **before** they happened, because everyone before us wrote them down afterwards, and always too late. Three of them are not solved at all: **the nodes may be wrong together for twenty-five years; the first cohort pays for our ignorance; and money does not save someone who has no choice.** Any project that tells you it has solved them **is lying** — and at that point it should be shut down. And the most dangerous thing in this list is **what is not in it**, and we will not learn what that is until someone is harmed and finds somewhere to say so. **Which is why the cheapness of that voice matters more than anything we have built here.**
