## 0. Money is not dirt — it is physics

**M6: a node must fund its own existence. No money, no node.**

This is neither harshness nor a cult of the market. It is structure:

**A system living off a donor has a hidden center above it.** State, patron, foundation, grant — it does not matter which: he who pays can stop paying, and on that day the node's independence turns out to have been paper. A donor is a **point of capture** disguised as a benefactor. He is not written into the charter, he is invisible in the graph, but he exists, and he decides.

Every high-minded system unable to feed itself **is already dead — it merely does not know it yet.** The project refuses to be one.

*How* to earn is **sovereign** (`03_SOVEREIGNTY`). What follows is not prescription but a set of instruments the project opens up, which a node is free to use or ignore.

## 1. Surrogacy: capitalism versus feudalism

**Surrogacy is legitimate everywhere and without reservation.** No node owes anyone an explanation for it, and the network holds no opinion on it. The grounding is not liberal but historical.

**The traditional model of reproduction was feudal.** Not metaphorically: a woman bore children because she **had no alternatives** — no education, no profession, no exit, no status apart from motherhood. Reproduction was **extracted** — by norm, by church, by economics, by the absence of choice. All of today's "successful" high-fertility models work the same way: they rest on the narrowing of a woman's alternatives.

**Modernity gave women alternatives — and they said no.** Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Europe. This is neither a vice nor a whim: it is exactly what happens when the seller acquires a choice.

**From here there are only two moves:**

1. **Feudal restoration** — take the alternatives back. This is what everyone whose fertility "works" does.
2. **The capitalist one** — **pay.**

The project chooses the second. **In what way was capitalism better than feudalism? In that you were paid for your labour rather than having it taken from you.** Exactly the same applies here. **Paying a woman to gestate is more dignified, not dirtier, than building a system that leaves her no choice.** Moralism at this point is simply the defence of feudalism with clean hands.

### And the boundary that follows from the same analogy

**Capitalism pays fairly only where the seller has alternatives.**

A worker with options negotiates; a worker without options is exploited, and payment becomes a **fig leaf on coercion.** That is the difference between an engineer and a sweatshop, and it is structural rather than moral: without alternatives there is no transaction — there is extraction.

Hence: **commercial surrogacy in a desperate population is feudalism that pays.** Not because money is dirty, but because there is no deal.

**Therefore the gestating woman gets a row in the protocol — the one actor who never had one:**

- **M8 extends to her.** Consent is **ongoing, not a one-time signature**: the right to change her mind, to exit, to renegotiate. She is a **participant in the node, not a vendor to it.**
- **M9 extends to her.** The long trace of gestating women: what became of them at 10, 20 years. A node that breaks them is **visible in the data** — by the same means as a node that breaks children.
- **The boundary criterion is universal** (`06_BOUNDARY` §9): the transaction must not collapse her into a pole — neither by taking away her own life and future (I) nor by isolating her from the world (J). She is a system with a boundary, like everyone.

## 2. The market: a grandfather orders a grandchild

The project's key customer group, and it must be described precisely, because intuition misleads everyone here.

### 2.1. Who the customer is

Men of 65–80. Wealthy. Often athletic and entirely capable of reproduction — spermatogenesis does not stop. The motive is not amusement: **the continuation of themselves.** They are invested in first-class upbringing and education — because these are **their children, not toys**, and because this is the last thing they will do.

### 2.2. Why the product is a grandchild, not a new family

The ordinary script of an ageing wealthy man: divorce, a young wife, a new family. Examine it in the project's language.

The wife he has had since eighteen has, over fifty years, **become part of him** — she is his accumulated **I**, a persistence the length of a life. An eighteen-year-old is a **stranger**, with whom nothing connects him. Trading the first for the second is not "a new life" but **burning one's own persistence for the sake of novelty**: buying pure J at the price of destroying I. This is collapse into the pole of chaos (`06_BOUNDARY`) — merely an expensive one. It usually ends the way it ends.

**The right product is not a replacement but an addition.**

> **Order yourself a grandchild.**

He **keeps his life** — the wife, the house, the half-century held in common — and **gains continuation.** I intact, J added. Nothing destroyed.

### 2.3. This is not an invention of the market but a restoration of the species norm

Humans are the **only species with a long post-reproductive life.** The best explanation of that fact (the grandmother hypothesis): **post-reproductive ancestors are more useful as raisers of grandchildren than as parents.** Menopause exists because a grandmother raising grandchildren raised the kin group's survival more than a grandmother bearing her own.

**The grandfather and grandmother *are* the allo-parent with whom the human being evolved.** They were killed by the isolated nuclear family: children grow up a thousand miles from the old, the old die of meaninglessness, and the mother alone carries what five people used to carry.

So **a seventy-year-old customer is not an aberration but a returning evolutionary norm.** The project is not inventing an unnatural market; it is **repairing an industrial-era breakage.**

### 2.4. A grandchild is really a grandchild

Not a metaphor and not a marketing frame. **The gametes are taken from the customer's children, not from the customer himself.**

The grandfather does not become a father at seventy-five. He obtains a **real grandchild** — from his son's sperm (or his daughter's eggs), gestated by a surrogate, raised by a node. The son, who **does not want to raise children**, nonetheless continues his line. The grandfather, who **wants to and can**, takes on everything else.

This is not a clarification of the product — it is a **major strengthening**, for four reasons.

**First, medical, and it is decisive.** The number of de novo mutations in sperm **rises with the father's age** — by current estimates, roughly **two additional mutations per year of the father's life**; paternal age is the dominant factor in a child's mutational load, with documented elevation of risk for several conditions. A seventy-five-year-old father transmits a load **several times greater** than a thirty-year-old.

> **This is the strongest medical objection to "old men fathering children." The grandchild framing removes it entirely.**

The sperm is the son's; he is thirty-five. The son's genome is already assembled and already clean. The grandfather pays; biology does not suffer.

**Second: genetic antitrust improves.** The grandfather contributes **25%, not 50%.** The same money, **half the concentration** of one genome in a population. He can have **more** grandchildren before the progressive price (§3) begins to bite. **Better for the customer and better for the gene pool at once** — a sure sign of a correct design.

**Third: the demographic argument becomes airtight.** The generation that **cannot hold the twenty-year form** (a shrunken I, `06_BOUNDARY` §8) continues its line all the same. The generation that **can** — retired, moneyed, motivated, time-rich — takes on the allo-parenting.

> **Foundation is a form-holding service for a generation that has forgotten how to hold forms.**

This is the grandmother hypothesis carried to its conclusion: the post-reproductive invest in offspring, the reproductive do not reproduce, and the project supplies precisely what is missing: **gestation and an apex.**

**Fourth: the child gets more people, not fewer.** Living parents (even if not raising him), living grandparents (paying and present), a permanent apex from the node. **A fuller kinship network than the late-life child of a dying man.**

### 2.4-bis. The grandmother — and here the model becomes human

There is an argument that outweighs the medical, the genetic and the demographic combined. It concerns **the wife.**

**A grandfather's late child is not her child.** A woman who has lived with him for fifty years is asked to raise **her husband's son by another woman.** That is a wound, and such wounds destroy marriages. No seventy-five-year-old will do this over his wife's head — and if he does, he will lose her.

**A grandchild is theirs together.** She is not a stepmother to a stranger's child — she is **the grandmother of her own grandchild**: fully, biologically, by right. **Far more pleasant** — and this is not sentiment but a condition of the deal existing at all.

> **Hence the essential point: the grandmother *is* the apex.**

She **wants** this. Evolution built her precisely for it (the grandmother hypothesis, §2.3). A healthy seventy-year-old woman has some seventeen or eighteen years ahead — enough for the critical years and most of childhood; a successor apex is mandatory in any case (M1, `05_ATTACHMENT` §4-bis).

**She is a living, motivated, biologically appropriate apex. She does not need to be "supplied."**

### 2.4-ter. The node is not a parent. The node is the village

The node's role thus becomes modest and clear: **infrastructure, allo-parents, education, AI, gestation, the successor apex, exchange with other nodes, management of money.**

> **The grandmother raises the grandchild. The node keeps the village.**

This is **the very village** the industrial nuclear family killed: children a thousand miles from the old, the old dying of meaninglessness, the mother alone carrying what five people used to carry.

**This is not a radical experiment — it is the restored human norm.** And it removes the chief horror the project evokes in an outsider: "an institution raising children." **No. A grandmother raises her grandchild. The institution keeps the village.**

### 2.4-quater. The gamete donor: briefly

The gametes belong to the son or daughter. **Consent is required, revocable up to fertilization. Paying is permitted and right.**

**That is all.** This is a five-minute act, not a kidney and not a pregnancy. The anti-coercion apparatus appropriate to the gestating woman (§1) is here an **error of proportion**: the son did not earn the inheritance, it was never his property, and "the unhappy billionaire's son forced to donate sperm" is an invented figure. Money here does exactly what money is for: **it makes "no" possible.**

### 2.4-quinquies. "My father could have, and did not" — not a risk but a disclosure requirement

The objection runs: the child will learn that a parent could have raised him and did not — and this will wound him.

**The mechanism of trauma is misunderstood here. Trauma requires a rupture in the narrative.** A child who learns at fifteen is wounded: his picture of the world collapses, a lie is discovered, a betrayal is discovered retroactively. A child who has known **from age zero**, matter-of-factly, the way one knows the colour of the sky, has **nothing to discover.** No revelation, no lie, no collapse. This accords with what is known about adoption and donor conception: **concealment wounds, not the fact.**

And the empirical point is simple: **millions of children around the world are right now being raised by grandparents** — with living parents who left, who work far away, who could not or would not. There are no demonstrations against this. It is normal, and it always was.

**So this is not a line in `17_RISKS` but a design requirement already satisfied by the openness principle: tell from age zero.** "That is dad. Grandma and grandpa raise me." Matter-of-factly, always, with no secret.

One honest residue, unin­flated: here the parent is absent **by choice** rather than by circumstance, and that is a slightly different fact to hold. But it is damped by the main one: **the child is fiercely wanted** — the grandfather paid, the grandmother raises him, the node invested. The wound of abandonment is "nobody wanted me." Here it is **precisely the reverse.**

**[question] What if the son changes his mind in year eight?** The apex remains the apex. A parent who appears later is **added, not substituted** (M1: permanence beats biology). This is not a punishment of the son — it is protection of the child from a second change of apex.

### 2.5. And M1 holds by itself

**The grandfather cannot be the attachment apex** — he will die when the child is ten or fifteen, and M1 requires the permanent figure to be designated **before the child appears** (`05_ATTACHMENT`).

But this is **not a limitation on the business model — it is its precise statement.** Grandmothers never were the attachment apex: they **do not replace the mother, they are added.** It was always so.

> **The grandfather buys continuation. The node supplies the apex.**

The roles do not conflict — they are exactly the roles humans have had for a hundred thousand years. And the seventy-year-old **does not want** to be up at night for eighteen years: he wants his line continued and raised superbly. **That is precisely what the node sells.**

## 2-bis. Corporations: Sony, TSMC, Samsung

The strongest route to market in the whole project — and the most dangerous. Taken apart.

### Why this is a market

South Korea's fertility rate is around **0.7** — the lowest ever recorded for a country at peace. Taiwan and Japan are close behind. Immigration there is **politically closed** — this is not Germany; a million people will not be brought in.

The consequence, understood in these companies better than anywhere: **TSMC needs engineers in 2050. There will not be enough Taiwanese engineers in 2050.** This is not a labour shortage — it is a civilizational one, and they have **no solution.**

East Asian corporations possess three things no one else does: **unlimited money, a twenty-five-year planning horizon, and no solution.** That is the definition of a market.

### And this is culturally native, not foreign

Japanese lifetime employment, the firm as an extended family, company housing, company sports. Korean chaebol are **literally family conglomerates.** Taiwanese family firms.

**The project is not selling them something alien.** It is selling them **their own tradition with a working reproductive engine attached** — something they once knew how to do, lost with the collapse of the bubble, and miss.

### The most dangerous sentence in the whole project

> "Children who with 70–80% probability will stay at your firm."

**You cannot sell it this way, and the argument is not moral.**

**Retention cannot be promised without either lying or capturing.** There is no third option. The capture version is to shape the child so that he **cannot want anything else**: narrow the alternatives, close off the world, leave one mode. This is **I-collapse** (`06_BOUNDARY`), industrialized. And it **violates M11**: a node that binds its graduate has captured a human being.

And here is the commercial killer of that version:

> **A person who cannot leave is a person who cannot think.**

Thinking requires J — letting the world in. A bound, rigid mind produces nothing new; it reproduces. **A semiconductor company does not need obedient bodies — it needs people who can think.** A captured engineer is a bad engineer, and TSMC will feel it before anyone.

**So the graduate's freedom is not an ethical constraint on the deal but its engineering specification.** M11 is sold to the corporation not as a concession but as a **quality requirement of the product.**

### Freedom is freedom: Sony loses 80% and wins

Here the objection inverts entirely.

**Sony will keep 20% of its own children. But it will receive 40% from TSMC and Samsung — if it deserves them.**

Corporations stop competing **for retention** and begin competing **for attraction.** This is a **fitness function on the employer** — and it is the **third domain of the same factory** (§5.3):

| Domain | Selection was | Selection becomes |
|---|---|---|
| Child-rearing | **blind** — no one knew what grew | M9, the long trace |
| Commerce | **bought** — the payer for attention won | an honest recommender |
| **Employment** | **frozen** — lifetime employment: you cannot leave, so the employer need not be good | **free graduates** |

**And this strikes East Asia exactly where it hurts.** Their labour market is famous for **illiquidity**: lifetime employment, seniority pay, almost no mid-career hiring. That freeze is a known drag on their innovation. **A company you cannot leave is a company that need not be good.** In the language of `06_BOUNDARY` this is the **I-collapse of the corporation**: one mode, the world does not enter, there is no renewal.

> **The project is not selling them "their own children." It is selling the first liquid market in engineering talent in East Asia** — populated by people who know the industry from childhood **and are free to leave.**

That is an incomparably larger product than retention. And it is **guaranteed by the second virtue** (`09_VIRTUES` §3): a child who lives each year in another way of life **physically cannot be captured** — he knows it can be otherwise not from stories but because he lived there.

### And this solves the free-rider problem

Alone, no corporation will fund children: **why pay to raise a competitor's employee?** A classic collective-action problem, and it would have killed the idea in the cradle.

**But if all fund, engineers exist at all** — and then everyone competes for the best **on merit**:

- the **public good** (the generation exists) is funded jointly;
- the **private good** (the best come to you) is won in competition.

And a free-riding corporation that **hires but does not fund** becomes visible in the graph — and in Japanese business culture the reputational cost of that is devastating. This is the same eucharistic communion applied to corporations: **not punishment, but withdrawal of recognition.**

And note: **Japan and Korea are culturally built for exactly this kind of task** — MITI, keiretsu, chaebol coordination. Coordinated industry-wide investment is their native strength, not an alien mechanism.

### What you actually sell — four things

**1. Existence.** In 2055 you will have engineers. Without this, you will not. That alone justifies the cheque.

**2. Affinity, not ownership.** A child raised **inside** your craft, among your people, where his grandfather worked — knows the trade, is good at it, has a home and ties there. Like a blacksmith's son in the forge. Such children enter the craft **disproportionately often** — a real base rate, observed in family firms, craft dynasties, military and academic families. **A high share — yes. A guarantee — never.** Sell the base rate, not the promise.

**3. Kinship.** The firm ceases to be a payroll and becomes a **line**: this is your son, grandson, brother, sister. Exactly what the Japanese firm considered itself to be before the bubble burst.

**4. Knowledge — and this is what makes the deal financeable today rather than in twenty-five years.**
A twenty-five-year payback is a hard sell even in Japan. But:

> **No one knows what a great engineer grows out of.**

Not one corporation in the world can answer that question — and all of them spend billions hiring blind. **The project's dataset (`08_DATA`) is the only thing capable of finding out.** TSMC will pay a fortune for knowledge that does not exist.

**The data pays from year one. The children from year twenty-five.** Sell both, and sell the knowledge first.

### Two safeguards, both structural

**The corporation must not become the node's hidden center.** A node living on Sony's money **has Sony above it**: on the day Sony says "change the upbringing or we withdraw," it will have no choice. That is exactly M6: **the payer is a point of capture.** Hence the cap on a single payer's share of node revenue (M6). The corporation is a **customer, not an owner.**

**And the child must know — from an early age and in plain words.**

> **Sony paid. Sony hopes. You are not obliged.**

To conceal this is to shape a human being **with an invisible hand he does not suspect.** That is a Second Foundation at the level of an individual soul — and it is the one thing the project swore not to be (`README`). Transparency here is not a courtesy but the openness principle applied to a person's own formation.

## 3. Genetic antitrust: progressive pricing and the diversity fund

A donor gets as many children as he wants. But **no skew may arise** (M7).

**The mechanism is not a ceiling but a progressive price with feedback:**

1. The price of a child from donor D in population P **rises superlinearly** with D's existing share of that population.
2. **The surcharge above base price does not stay with the node** — it goes into a **diversity fund** subsidizing children from under-represented genomes **in that same population.**
3. Result: **every additional child of a dominant donor finances the children of his competitors.** His own money buys his counterweight.

**Why a price and not a ceiling:** on a billionaire a ceiling is a prohibition, while a price is no obstacle — he will pay any. But if his surcharge automatically funds rival genomes, **the share physically cannot run away** — it damps itself the harder the faster it grows. The rule is self-correcting: no enforcement, no center.

**Why this is needed at all — three mechanical arguments, not one moral:**

- **A clonal colony.** A hundred thousand half-siblings in one population is one lock for all; a parasite that finds the key cuts them all down at once. Monoculture is fragile (`04_EVOLUTION`).
- **Inbreeding.** Half-siblings will meet without knowing it. Ordinary sperm banks impose per-donor limits for exactly this reason — mechanics, not morality.
- **Capture.** Whoever owns the network's gene pool owns the network. That is an M7 bypass through the genome.

### The distributed parentage registry

Shares are tracked **without a center**: nodes publish hashed donor identifiers and counters; **anyone** can compute a share; **no one** owns the registry. Exactly like the communion graph (`02_COMMUNION` §4) — not a document but the result of reading.

A node that does not apply the progression to itself **loses communion.**

## 4. The AI as Linux

**The Foundation AI is open source.** Not a service, not a cloud, not a subscription to someone else's brain.

- **Families and organizations run their own.** Your hardware, your weights, your rules.
- **You control everything.** **Your** AIs work. **Your** children grow.
- **The network shares only the protocol for exchanging numbers** (M4) — but **never the model.**

**Why this is not ideology but M7.** A single AI across the network is **a hidden center**, a Second Foundation through the back door: an anti-monopoly bypass not through the charter, not through money, not through the genome, but **through the model.** Whoever holds the one model that shapes all the network's children holds the network, however its charter reads.

And scientifically: a single model **homogenizes the environment** and destroys between-node variation — the very thing for which all this exists (`08_DATA`).

**Indexes are plural too.** A local AI cannot crawl the whole internet; it needs a shared layer of knowledge about the world and about products. The same logic applies: **many competing indexes, like package repositories.** Everyone chooses which to trust; anyone may run one; no one owns them all. Linux all the way down, repositories included.

## 5. The recommender: a new turn of evolution

Here the project ceases to be a project about children and becomes a second project of the same magnitude.

### 5.1. There is no neutral information

A mother sits at home and asks her AI: **I want goat cheese, but strictly local. What do you recommend?**

And it recommends. It knows the reviews, the prices, it watches the net, it knows more precisely than anyone. It tells **the truth.**

**And it still pointed at someone.**

**Information is already advertising.** The choice cannot be evaded: silence is also a choice — to abandon the family to the existing market, where the recommendation was bought long ago. **Refusing to recommend is not innocence but cowardice dressed as cleanliness.**

### 5.2. So the question was never "whether to advertise" but what advertising SELECTS FOR

**Today advertising is the Goodhart of the economy.** The winner is not the best product but the one who paid most for attention. The firm optimizes **attention capture instead of the product**; one mode crowds out the rest; the product stops improving. In the language of `06_BOUNDARY` this is the **I-collapse of business**: cancer, industrialized, winning inside the organism of the economy and killing it.

**Under the protocol, the winner is the one who is actually better.**

> **Advertising does not disappear. It acquires an honest fitness function.**

Information in the form of **matching** is not persuasion but **selection.** The seller can no longer win by shouting louder. He can win only by **being better.** This is the new impetus for business: **an evolution that never existed in commerce, because its signal was bought.**

### 5.3. This is the same move as M9. Foundation is a fitness-function factory

| Domain | Selection was | Selection becomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Child-rearing** | blind: no one knew what grew | **M9, the long trace** — the signal is the grown human; a node's marketing cannot save it |
| **Commerce** | bought: the payer for attention won | **an honest recommender** — the signal is the truth about the product; a seller's marketing cannot save him |

**The same move.** The project builds **honest signals of selection where selection was blind or bought.** That is its true nature — not a network of children's homes but a **factory of fitness functions**, launching evolution in segments of culture where there was none.

### 5.4. Goodhart returns one level up — and is cured by the same medicine

**The danger is real:** if the recommender judges by reviews and metrics, sellers will optimize the **metrics** rather than the product. Reviews are already gamed. Any published measure of quality will be hacked within the quarter.

**The cure is an exact copy of M9: the long trace of the purchase.**

The family's AI knows not the review but the **fact**: what was bought, what is actually used, what was thrown out after a month, and — above all — **whether they are satisfied a year later.** This is a signal that **cannot be gamed because it is not published**: it is distributed across millions of private AIs belonging to families, and only de-identified numbers leave (M4).

**The recommender's fitness function = the long trace of the purchase.** No proxy — an observed outcome. Goodhart dies for the same reason as in M9: **there is nothing to hack.**

### 5.5. The instrument must be unbuyable — a metrological argument, not a moral one

**A scale that can be bought is not a scale.**

If a seller can pay for placement in a recommendation, the AI **ceases to be an instrument of truth** and becomes an instrument of the seller. And then it ceases to be a fitness function — and becomes once again what advertising already is: **noise.**

> **Kill the instrument and you kill the evolution you have just launched.**

This is not ethical squeamishness. It is the sole condition under which the whole construction of §5.2 works at all.

### 5.6. Then where the money comes from — and it is worth more than advertising

A seller legitimately wants three things: **to be found, to be measured, and to learn why he lost.** Sell him the third.

**1. Feedback — selling the fitness signal back to the organisms.**
"Here is why you were not chosen. Here is what people actually wanted. Here is where you lost and where you won." This is **business intelligence that does not exist today**: not a survey, not a focus group, not a panel — **the real behaviour of real families, de-identified and aggregated.** It is worth more than any advertising — and, unlike advertising, it **accelerates evolution instead of corrupting it.** You are selling business **eyesight.**

**2. Measurement and certification.**
A seller pays to have his product **tested** and entered into the index. **He pays for measurement — not for placement.** He can buy the test; **he cannot buy the grade.** The method of measurement is open (M10) — anyone can check it.

**3. A flat listing fee.**
If presence is charged for, the charge is **identical for all and has no effect on rank.** A commission that depends on whose product is recommended corrupts the objective function the same day.

### 5.7. Whose AI — and a correction to an earlier restriction

**The AI is the agent of its owner. Always.** The mother's AI belongs to the mother. **The child's AI belongs to the child — from age zero; the child knows it, plays with it, erases it** (`08_DATA` §2).

As the child's agent, the AI may honestly say: "this toy is well made and cheap." It **serves the child** — that is its job.

What it may **never** do: **take money from the toymaker.**

> **One universal rule: a recommendation cannot be bought — not from a six-year-old and not from a forty-six-year-old.**

The thing to restrict is **not the addressee but the purchase.** The earlier rule — "the child is never the addressee of a recommendation" — was superfluous and wrong: it protected the child from his own agent.

## 7. Honest limits

- **[risk] The recommender itself becomes a monopoly.** Cured only by a plurality of indexes (§4, the repository model). One index = one center = death. This must be guarded more closely than anything else.
- **[risk] Certification degenerates into a fee for entry.** Mitigated by certification **not affecting rank** and by the method being open. But the pressure will be constant.
- **[limit] The long trace of a purchase takes time.** In the first years the recommender is blind and must lean on public metrics — that is, on what is already gamed. **The start is necessarily worse than maturity**, and there is no way around it.
- **[limit] The grandfather dies.** The node must outlive the customer: money up front, the node's will (M12), the catch (M5). A child must not depend on how long the person who commissioned him happens to live.
- **[hypothesis] That an honest recommender will beat a bought one.** Not a fact. Bought advertising has a thousandfold budget. The bet — that **truth is more useful than lies to whoever pays for outcomes** — is strong but untested.
