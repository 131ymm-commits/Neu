# 12. JURISDICTION

### Where this is legal · Who is the legal guardian · Why the window is closing

> **Not legal advice.** This is a strategic map. Every node must retain local counsel; not a line below substitutes for it.

---

## 0. As designed, the project is illegal in half of Europe

And this is **not an objection but a briefing.** The law does not ask whether an idea is good. It determines **where to begin** — and nothing more.

---

## 1. The 2026 map: the window is closing, not opening

**Total ban on surrogacy:** France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Switzerland, Finland, Slovakia (written **into the constitution**, 2025), China, Japan.
**Altruistic only:** Canada, United Kingdom, Australia, Greece, South Africa, Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark, Brazil. **Commercial surrogacy is banned in all 27 EU states.**
**Commercial permitted (including for foreigners):** certain US states — **California** (pre-birth parentage orders **regardless of nationality, marital status or orientation** — the most protected regime in the world), Nevada, Illinois, Connecticut, Colorado, New York; Georgia (foreigners, heterosexual couples — **but a ban is pending in parliament**); Ukraine (war); Mexico (some states); Colombia; Kazakhstan; Iran (married heterosexual couples only).

**The trend since 2020 is unambiguously restrictive:**

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 2021 | India — altruistic only, citizens only |
| 2022–23 | Russia — ban for foreigners |
| **Oct 2024** | **Italy: a "universal crime"** — an Italian is prosecuted **at home** for surrogacy **anywhere in the world**, in the same category as human trafficking and genocide |
| 2025 | Greece — residency requirement; Spain — tightened registration of children born abroad; Slovakia — constitutional ban |
| Nov 2025 | Kenya — ban for foreigners, **up to 10 years' imprisonment** |
| **Mar 2026** | **The Hague Conference decided not to advance the surrogacy convention** — international harmonization **is off** (it may return in 2028) |

**Two conclusions, both strategic.**

**First: there will be no harmonization. The patchwork is permanent.** The project cannot be designed for a future unified regime — none is coming.

**And oddly, that is good: the law is already polycentric.** There is no world legislature, no single center — only a multitude of incompatible jurisdictions that recognize or do not recognize one another. **That is precisely a communion graph, only for states.** The network maps onto legal reality **exactly**, instead of fighting it.

**Second: the window is closing, and that is arithmetic, not rhetoric.** Every year of delay is **one fewer jurisdiction**. "We should have started yesterday" is now confirmed by the statute books.

## 2. The masterkey: the node **never** holds custody of a child

The most important section of this file.

The temptation is to make the node the legal guardian: a "children's village," an "institution of care." **This is a dead end for two reasons at once.**

**Legally:** residential custody of children is **licensed** almost everywhere, subject to inspection, requires a special status, and in half the world is simply closed to a foreign organization.

**In substance:** a node that holds custody **is SOS** (`10_PRECEDENTS`) — an institution that owns a child. We have already rejected that.

**The correct construction:**

> **The legal parent is the apex. The child lives with her. The node is housing, a school, services, software.**

**The grandmother (or whoever the apex is) is the legal guardian.** *Kinship care* is recognized **almost everywhere**: the most ordinary, boring, well-worn legal form there is. Millions of children worldwide live exactly this way.

**The node legally becomes:**

- a housing cooperative or developer,
- a private school,
- a care-and-services company,
- a software developer.

**Not one new legal category. Nothing exotic to license.** All of it is boring and legal in nearly every country on earth.

> **The legal form has converged with the moral one: the node is the village, not the parent.**

---

## 3. The apex is not an employee. The apex is a legal parent

`01_PROTOCOL`, M1: **the apex leaves only together with the child.** How is that constituted in law?

**Not by an employment contract.** An eighteen-year employment contract is **unenforceable and, in substance, unlawful** almost everywhere: it is indenture. A worker cannot be bound for eighteen years, and any court will void it.

**The only workable form is parental legal status:**

- **a relative (grandmother, grandfather, parent)** — the status already exists; nothing to constitute;
- **a non-relative** — **adoption or long-term guardianship.** He or she must become a **legal parent**, not an employee.

And then M1 stops being a rule of the protocol and becomes **the definition of parenthood**:

> **A parent who leaves takes the child. Because that is what being a parent means.**

She cannot be dismissed (parents are not dismissed). She cannot walk away (leaving means taking the child). If she harms the child, **the state removes her under criminal and family law**, and the successor steps in (M1). **The network builds no parallel justice system** (`02_COMMUNION` §8.4).

## 4. Surrogacy is an option, not a foundation

**The core of the project works without surrogacy at all.**

Village + apex + allo-parents + AI + data + exchange + the long trace = **natural reproduction by ordinary families.** Legal **everywhere.**

Surrogacy is needed for exactly one scenario — **the grandfather orders a grandchild** (`07_ECONOMY` §2) — and only where it is lawful.

**And here is the consequence that removes half the problem:**

> **Corporate nodes in Japan, Korea and Taiwan require no surrogacy whatsoever.**

Sony pays **so that its employees have children**: housing, a village, grandmothers, a school, an AI, money. Natural reproduction, kinship guardianship, a private school. **Legal in Japan, Korea and Taiwan — even though surrogacy there is banned.**

**The project's principal market does not collide with the project's principal legal barrier.** This should have been noticed sooner.

## 5. School

**Homeschooling is banned** in Germany (strictly), and nearly so in Sweden and the Netherlands.

**But a private school is legal almost everywhere** — including in Germany, where what is banned is homeschooling, not private schools.

**The node runs a school, not "home education."** The barrier is passed not by cleverness but by using the ordinary form.

---

## 6. Data is an asset, not a burden

Children's data is normally regarded as a legal nightmare. Here it is **the reverse.**

| Regulator's requirement | What Foundation does |
|---|---|
| **Right to erasure** (GDPR Art. 17) | **The erasure game** (`09_VIRTUES` §2) — Article 17 **turned into a children's game**, exercised daily rather than by written request |
| **Data minimization** (Art. 5) | A local AI; **only numbers** leave; the raw never leaves the child (`08_DATA` §7) |
| **The data belongs to the subject** | The child's data belongs to **the child**, and he knows it from age zero |
| **Transparency of processing** | The method is open (M10); the recommendation logic is open |
| **Consent** | The child's consent — from the moment he can speak (`08_DATA` §6) |

> **Foundation is more GDPR-compliant than any existing children's service.**

This is not grudging compliance — it is a **competitive moat and an argument in front of a regulator.** COPPA (US, under 13) is covered by the same design.

## 7. A red line: a weak state is not a refuge but a **signal**

**SOS's worst abuses were in Africa and Asia** (`10_PRECEDENTS` §1). A weak state = weak inspection = impunity = abuse. That is not coincidence; it is mechanism.

**The network is not the police** (`02_COMMUNION` §8.4). Therefore **the state must be.** The project needs a country with a **working** legal system, not one without.

> **Choosing a jurisdiction for the weakness of its oversight is itself a sign of drift and grounds for breaking communion.**

A node that relocates to where no one will check on it **has told you everything you need to know.** In the graph this must read as a red flag, not as cost optimization.

---

## 8. Jurisdictional diversity = antifragility

**All nodes in one country + an Italian-style reversal = the network dies in a day.**

Nodes across ten jurisdictions — **no single government can kill the network.** It can kill its own node; the children are caught by others (M5, M12); the graph lives on.

> **This is M7 applied to law.** Concentration in one jurisdiction is the same monopoly, only a governmental one.

And it is **another axis of variance**, which `11_START` §3 already demands: the first three nodes — **different faiths, different founders, different money, different jurisdictions.**

---

## 9. Where to begin

**Requirements for the first jurisdiction:**

1. **A working legal system and working child protection** (point 7 — this is a requirement, not a cost);
2. kinship care / adoption as an established form;
3. private schooling lawful;
4. data law workable (GDPR is workable);
5. political stability on a 25-year horizon;
6. surrogacy — **only if that node uses it.**

**Candidates for the first three (they must differ):**

| Jurisdiction | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| **USA (California, Colorado)** | Surrogacy — the most protected regime in the world; private schools; kinship care; rule of law | Expensive; political volatility |
| **Canada** | Strong rule of law; altruistic surrogacy; a different legal family | Commercial surrogacy closed |
| **United Kingdom** | Strong rule of law; altruistic surrogacy; language | Parental order only **after** birth; months |
| **Israel** | Surrogacy open to all since 2022; a strong grandmother culture; technology | Geopolitical risk |
| **Czechia / Estonia / Portugal** | EU; surrogacy unregulated or workable; cheap | Grey zones — risk of reversal |
| **Japan / Korea / Taiwan** | **The principal market** (`07_ECONOMY` §2-bis); surrogacy **not needed** (§4) | Surrogacy banned; heavy bureaucracy |

**A sensible first three:** one node in the USA (the surrogacy anchor), one in East Asia (corporate, **without surrogacy**), one in Europe or Canada (a different legal family). **Three jurisdictions, three faiths, three sources of money.**

## 10. Honest limits

- **[unsolvable] Italian-style extraterritorial criminalization.** If the model spreads, **participants become criminals at home** regardless of where the node operates. There is no way around it — only not to have nodes that depend on one country.
- **[unsolvable] Capture by a state.** The Syrian case at SOS: the state takes the children and the network can do nothing. M5 and M12 work **only if the state releases them.** Acknowledged; carried into `17_RISKS`.
- **[expensive] Cross-border recognition of parentage.** Months, courts, immigration. The Hague convention **collapsed in March 2026** — it will not get easier.
- **[risk] Georgia.** A ban is pending in parliament. Building on it is building on sand.
- **[unknown] Regulators' reaction to a children's AI.** No data exists: no such product exists. It may prove far easier (§6) or catastrophically harder.

---

**The formula of this file.** The law does not ask an idea's permission — it assigns it a place. And a place exists: **the node never takes custody, the grandmother remains the legal parent, and the node becomes, in law, a housing cooperative with a school** — a form so boring that it is legal almost everywhere. Surrogacy is an option, not a foundation: the project's principal market in East Asia has no need of it at all. And the fact that the window closes year by year — Italy, Kenya, Slovakia, the collapse of the Hague project in March 2026 — means exactly one thing: **"we should have started yesterday" is confirmed not by rhetoric but by the statute books.**
